## B. News and report signals

1. **LA County life-expectancy gap 16.3 years (Measure of America, Mar 2026).** Ties income/HDI to longevity at neighbourhood level, and Latino life expectancy fell 3.7 years since 2017. *Why:* flagship US city example of the poverty–health gradient. *Next owner:* Lead (decide on S7), then Curator. Note: some outlets say the gap is "widening". Check that against the report's own comparison before reusing that word.
2. **London Health Profile 2026 (Oct 2026).** Men in the most deprived areas live nearly 8 fewer years than men in the least deprived. Kensington & Chelsea ward gap: 17 years for men (Queen's Gate vs Notting Dale, borough JSNA); a secondary article reports 19 years for women. *Why:* European flagship example. Figures come from different years and measures, so **do not combine them**. *Next owner:* Lead → Collector (S5/S6).
3. **2025 ACS 1-year estimates scheduled for release 2026-10-29.** *Why:* fresh city poverty and uninsured rates. The health-insurance question changed, which breaks the series. *Next owner:* Collector (S2), Curator (series-break flag).
4. **NYC Medicaid/SNAP cuts (OBBBA).** FWD.us: New York loses about $12.2B in federal health and food support by 2029. NYC Comptroller (2025 projection): about 800k NYC residents may lose Medicaid. Public Health Solutions: about 1M. Sep 2026 reporting (Brownstoner/THE CITY) describes more food-pantry reliance. *Why:* links food insecurity and health-coverage loss in the largest US city. These are **projections from different methods that disagree**. Track the measured outcomes instead (NY State of Health / HRA enrolment data). *Next owner:* Lead (whether to watch NYC enrolment data as a source).
5. **Eurostat unmet health care needs (2024 data; update planned Dec 2026).** *Why:* EU-wide income gradient in access to care. Watch for the Dec 2026 update. *Next owner:* Scout (watch), Collector (S4 if approved).

Out of scope, noted and dropped: Bristol, Leeds and Northern Ireland/Scotland deprivation reports (below 2M or not city-level).
